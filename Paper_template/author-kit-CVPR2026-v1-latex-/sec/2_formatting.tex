\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Frequency-domain detectors.} The frequency-domain approach to fake-image detection predates the current wave of diffusion models. Durall \etal~\cite{durall2020watch} showed that GAN upsampling layers leave behind a characteristic deviation from the $1/f^{\alpha}$ power law of natural images, and used the azimuthally averaged power spectrum as a near-linearly-separable feature. Zhang \etal~\cite{zhang2019detecting} proposed a spectral-based classifier targeting GAN checkerboard artifacts, and Frank \etal~\cite{frank2020leveraging} trained CNNs directly on DCT coefficients. The high-level lesson from this line of work is that the discrete upsampling operators used inside generators leak energy into specific bands of the spectrum, which generalises across generators built from the same primitives. Diffusion models are different beasts, so it is not obvious that these results transfer; Corvi \etal~\cite{corvi2023intriguing} and Ricker \etal~\cite{ricker2024aeroblade} report that they partially do, while Synthbuster~\cite{synthbuster2023} and SPAI~\cite{spai2025} push frequency-only detection to current diffusion benchmarks with competitive results. Our work is closest in spirit to these; we differ in that we run a controlled 1D-versus-2D comparison rather than committing to either representation.

\paragraph{Semantic / learned-feature detectors.} A second family of detectors uses pretrained visual encoders (CLIP~\cite{radford2021clip}, DINOv2~\cite{oquab2023dinov2}) as frozen feature extractors and trains a shallow classifier on top~\cite{ojha2023univfd,cozzolino2024raising}. These methods generalise impressively well but are expensive at inference, inherit biases from their backbones, and are difficult to interpret. Wang \etal~\cite{wang2020cnn} is the canonical pixel-space CNN detector and showed that training on a single GAN generalises to many others; we view our work as the frequency-domain analogue of that paper, transplanted to the diffusion era.

\paragraph{Robustness and dataset artifacts.} Several recent papers show that detector performance on public benchmarks is confounded by post-processing~\cite{fakeorjpeg2024,grommelt2024}: in particular, GenImage~\cite{genimage} happens to differ in JPEG quality between its real and fake classes, which means naively trained detectors can score above 0.9 AUC while essentially learning to classify compression quality. Any frequency-domain detector is especially vulnerable to this, because the frequency distortions induced by JPEG quantisation are themselves a dominant signal. Following~\cite{fakeorjpeg2024}, we treat GenImage results with explicit caution, report JPEG-robustness curves (planned; see Section~\ref{sec:experiments}), and keep Defactify~\cite{defactify} as a less-JPEG-confounded multi-generator benchmark alongside it.

\paragraph{Adversarial attacks on detectors.} PGD~\cite{madry2018pgd} is the standard first-order attack for evaluating classifier robustness. Most prior attacks on AI-image detectors operate in pixel space~\cite{carlini2020evading}; relatively few attack the detector directly in its input domain. Because our detector's input \emph{is} the Fourier transform of the image, a pixel-space attack and a frequency-space attack are related by a linear change of variables but differ in which perturbations look most natural and which bands are cheapest to modify. We exploit this to turn PGD into an interpretability probe: the attacker's preferred spectral coordinates are, by construction, the coordinates the detector most relies on.

\paragraph{Datasets.} CIFAKE~\cite{cifake} pairs CIFAR-10 photographs with Stable Diffusion v1.4 images of the same classes and has become a standard small-scale benchmark. GenImage~\cite{genimage} is the main diffusion-era multi-generator dataset, covering eight generators (ADM, BigGAN, GLIDE, Midjourney, SD v1.4, SD v1.5, VQDM, Wukong) with ImageNet real photographs. GenImage++~\cite{genimagepp} extends the protocol with harder, more recent generators (Flux, multi-style variants) and is intended as a test-only holdout. Defactify~\cite{defactify} is a mixture benchmark distributed as HuggingFace parquet shards. FaceForensics++~\cite{ffpp} remains the dominant face-manipulation benchmark and is largely orthogonal to the present work, though we discuss its integration as future work.
