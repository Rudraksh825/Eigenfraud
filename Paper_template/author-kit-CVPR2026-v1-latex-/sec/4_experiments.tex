\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We ran three experiments, in increasing order of dataset scale and complexity: CIFAKE (single generator, balanced), Defactify (multi-generator mixture), and a four-generator subset of GenImage. All three are completed; Table~\ref{tab:results} summarises the numbers.

\subsection{Exploratory Analysis}
\label{sec:eda}

Before training, we check that class-level spectral differences are actually present. Following~\cite{durall2020watch}, we compute the mean 2D log-power spectrum $\bar{S}_{\text{real}}(u,v)$ and $\bar{S}_{\text{fake}}(u,v)$ over balanced samples from each class and look at the residual $\Delta S(u,v) = \bar{S}_{\text{fake}}(u,v) - \bar{S}_{\text{real}}(u,v)$ (Figure~\ref{fig:eda_spectra}). On CIFAKE, $\Delta S$ is visibly structured rather than noise: fake images have a systematic energy excess in a mid-frequency annulus and a slight deficit at the highest frequencies, which is consistent with a decoder that smooths fine texture. The same gap appears in the 1D profiles. This is a basic sanity check --- it tells us a CNN should be able to separate the classes, but not how well or whether the pattern will generalise.

\subsection{CIFAKE: Single-Generator Baseline}
\label{sec:cifake}

\paragraph{Setup.} CIFAKE~\cite{cifake} contains 60k real CIFAR-10 photographs and 60k Stable Diffusion v1.4 generations in a pre-existing 50k/10k train/test split per class. We train both models from scratch on the train split with default hyperparameters (Section~\ref{sec:training}) and evaluate on the vendor test split.

\paragraph{Results.} \textsc{cnn2d} reaches a best validation AUC of 0.9525 at epoch 13 and plateaus afterwards; \textsc{cnn1d} reaches 0.9399 at epoch 25. Both comfortably beat chance using no pixel features. The 2D model's advantage is small (+0.013 AUC), which makes sense: a single generator produces a single artifact pattern, and the isotropic component is already a near-complete signal.

\paragraph{A note on reproducibility.} An earlier run of the same configuration reached 0.9650 (\textsc{cnn2d}) and 0.9449 (\textsc{cnn1d}), but those checkpoints were lost. The numbers above are from a retrain. The best epoch is the same between runs (epoch 13 for the 2D model), which suggests the model hits a ceiling for this dataset rather than that either run was unusually lucky or unlucky.

\subsection{Defactify: Multi-Generator Mixture}
\label{sec:defactify}

\paragraph{Setup.} Defactify~\cite{defactify} is distributed as HuggingFace parquet shards and contains images from a mixture of modern generators with a fixed train/validation split. We read the parquet files directly via a lightweight \texttt{ParquetFrequencyDataset} class that decodes the embedded image bytes and applies the same preprocessing as in Section~\ref{sec:preproc}.

\paragraph{Results.} \textsc{cnn2d} reaches a best validation AUC of 0.9049 at epoch 11; \textsc{cnn1d} reaches 0.8439 at epoch 29. The key observation here is that moving from a single-generator benchmark (CIFAKE) to a multi-generator mixture (Defactify) increases the gap between the two models from +0.013 to +0.061 AUC --- roughly a $5\times$ increase. The 1D model still works well above chance (the isotropic fingerprint is real), but the 2D model's advantage grows, which is what you would expect if different generators have different anisotropic artifacts that averaging azimuthally washes out.

\subsection{GenImage: Four-Generator Subset}
\label{sec:genimage}

\paragraph{Setup.} The full GenImage benchmark~\cite{genimage} covers eight generators, but due to practical constraints (inode limits on the storage volume we used, incomplete archive extraction) we only trained on four: ADM, BigGAN, VQDM, and GLIDE. Real images come from the ImageNet-1k validation set (50k images). The combined dataset has approximately 50k real and 304k fake images --- a roughly 1:6 imbalance that we handle with inverse-frequency class weighting in the loss. We train with on-the-fly FFT on a single GPU (H100), pre-compute spectra to sharded \texttt{.npz} files for the cached training path, and evaluate on a held-out 10\% test split from \texttt{make\_splits()}.

\paragraph{Results.} \textsc{cnn2d} reaches a test AUC of \textbf{0.9990}, accuracy 98.18\%, and EER 1.02\%. \textsc{cnn1d} reaches a best validation AUC of \textbf{0.9375} at epoch 29 (a separate test-split eval for the 1D model was not run). The gap between the two models on this benchmark is roughly consistent with what we saw on Defactify.

\paragraph{Important caveat.} The 0.9990 AUC for \textsc{cnn2d} should not be taken at face value. Grommelt~\etal~\cite{grommelt2024} have shown that GenImage has a systematic JPEG quality difference between its real images (ImageNet JPEG-encoded photographs) and its fake images (PNG or high-quality JPEG generations), which means a frequency-domain detector can achieve high accuracy largely by classifying compression quality. We have not controlled for this --- we ran standard training without any JPEG-matching preprocessing. So the GenImage result tells us the detector \emph{can} separate these two distributions, but not whether it is detecting something specific to the generators or just picking up on the compression difference. A proper evaluation would require re-encoding both splits at matched JPEG quality, which we leave as future work.

We include two qualitative interpretability checks. First, Figure~\ref{fig:eda_spectra} shows the class-mean spectra and confirms that the spectral differences the CNN is learning from are real and structured, not noise. Second, Figure~\ref{fig:gradcam} shows Grad-CAM~\cite{selvaraju2017gradcam} heatmaps for \textsc{cnn2d} on individual examples: the model's gradient attention concentrates in the mid-frequency annulus, consistent with the EDA and with prior work~\cite{durall2020watch} on where generator artifacts tend to live. These are illustrative rather than rigorous, but they help build a story for why the 2D model outperforms the 1D model on diverse generators: the anisotropic mid-frequency structure is not fully captured by a radial average.
