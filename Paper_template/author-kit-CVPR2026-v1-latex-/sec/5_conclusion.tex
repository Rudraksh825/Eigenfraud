\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\paragraph{What the results support.}
The completed evidence supports a simple conclusion: benchmark construction artifacts explain a substantial share of current detector behavior. The clean benchmark, CIFAKE, does not reward these shortcuts, and almost every detector fails there in the strongest possible way: below chance. The shortcut-rich benchmark, GenImage, restores strong performance for the same detectors. Defactify falls in between, and mostly produces near-chance behavior.

\paragraph{Why the below-chance result matters.}
The most useful empirical lesson in this paper is that AUC $<0.5$ should be treated as a qualitative signal, not just a bad score. Repeated below-chance performance on an artifact-controlled benchmark indicates that the model has learned a stable but incorrect rule. That makes CIFAKE valuable not because it is perfect, but because it exposes inversion clearly.

\paragraph{Implications for evaluation.}
Claims about AI-image detection should not rely on a single benchmark or on high AUC alone. At minimum, a detector should be tested on:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item a benchmark with tightly controlled format and resolution,
  \item a counter-benchmark where the usual shortcut is absent or reversed,
  \item and a normalized version of the same data to see whether performance survives re-encoding.
\end{itemize}
Without that structure, it is too easy to confuse benchmark reward hacking with actual forensic capability.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
This draft uses the experiments that were complete at writing time. GenImage normalization and frequency-band ablation were still in progress and are therefore not needed for the main argument here. We also evaluate released checkpoints rather than retraining every method on artifact-controlled data.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
The paper's story is not that AI-image detection is impossible. It is that current benchmark numbers are too easy to inflate. Across three datasets, spectral separation grows with construction bias; detector inversion appears when shortcuts disappear; and detector success returns when those shortcuts are put back. That pattern is more consistent with learning the benchmark than learning the generator.
