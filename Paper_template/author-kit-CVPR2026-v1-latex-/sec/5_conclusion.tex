\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\paragraph{What we can already claim.} On two diffusion-era benchmarks, a purely frequency-domain CNN with no pretrained weights, no semantic features, and no data augmentation learns to separate real from generated images at a level substantially above chance. On a single-generator benchmark (CIFAKE) the isotropic and anisotropic representations are within $0.013$ AUC of each other; on a multi-generator mixture (Defactify) the gap grows to $0.061$ AUC in favour of the full 2D representation. This is a small amount of evidence, but it points in a specific and testable direction: the frequency-domain fingerprint of diffusion models has a sizeable directional component, and collapsing to a radial profile --- the canonical move in the GAN-era frequency-detector literature --- leaves signal on the table as soon as the generator pool stops being a single model.

\paragraph{What we still need to show.} The GenImage leave-one-generator-out and JPEG robustness experiments (E1, E2) are the tests that separate a plausible hypothesis from a load-bearing claim, and they are in progress. The Fourier-space PGD attack (E3) is designed to give a second, independent answer to the same question as Grad-CAM: if both converge on the same annulus of spectral bins, that is stronger evidence about the detector's internal reasoning than either provides alone. We will report all three regardless of whether the results are favourable; in particular, if LOGO AUC on GenImage collapses near chance, the honest conclusion is that the frequency-only story is weaker in the diffusion era than it was in the GAN era, and we will say so.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The method is constrained to whole-image classification and cannot spatially localise a manipulated region --- the Fourier transform of a full image is global. It is also by construction blind to semantic implausibility (wrong number of fingers, nonsensical text) that a foundation-model-based detector would catch for free. We view these as features rather than bugs in the context of this paper (frequency-only detection is worth studying on its own terms), but the honest framing for deployment is as one branch of an ensemble rather than a standalone oracle. Finally, the Defactify result is reported on the vendor validation split and inherits any biases of that split; we do not yet have a clean cross-dataset generalisation measurement for it.

\paragraph{Conclusion.} Frequency-domain detection is not obsolete in the diffusion era, but the simple azimuthally averaged power spectrum that characterised the GAN-era literature is an increasingly lossy summary: the full 2D log-power spectrum carries enough additional directional information to justify its extra parameters, and the gap between the two grows with generator diversity. The Eigenfraud architectures are intentionally small, architecturally transparent, and reproducible; the infrastructure for the harder experiments is in place; and the next iteration of this paper will report whether the hypothesis survives contact with the full GenImage benchmark and with a targeted spectral adversary.
