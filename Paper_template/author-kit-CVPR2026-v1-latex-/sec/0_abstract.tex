\begin{abstract}
Generative image models leave behind spectral fingerprints: upsampling stages, learned decoders, and the spectral bias of neural networks all deposit characteristic energy at specific spatial frequencies, even when the resulting pixel content is photorealistic. We ask whether the frequency-only detection paradigm that worked for GANs~\cite{wang2020cnn,durall2020watch} still holds in the diffusion era, and whether the choice of spectral representation matters. We introduce \textbf{Eigenfraud}, a detector that operates entirely in the Fourier domain: every image is mapped to its 2D log-power spectrum, and two lightweight CNNs are trained on complementary views of it --- a 1D CNN on the azimuthally averaged radial profile (isotropic structure only, $\sim$180k parameters) and a 2D CNN on the full centred spectrum (isotropic and anisotropic structure, $\sim$4M parameters). Neither model touches pixels; neither uses pretrained weights or semantic features. On CIFAKE~\cite{cifake}, our 2D model reaches a validation AUC of 0.953 and the 1D model 0.940, confirming that even a narrow radial summary is enough to distinguish Stable Diffusion v1.4 images from real CIFAR-10 photographs. On the Defactify~\cite{defactify} multi-generator benchmark, the same architectures reach 0.905 (2D) and 0.844 (1D) AUC, showing that the gap between the two representations widens once generators become more diverse, consistent with the hypothesis that directional artifacts carry additional discriminative signal. We further describe a planned cross-generator generalisation study on the full GenImage~\cite{genimage} benchmark and a frequency-space PGD attack~\cite{madry2018pgd} designed to reveal which spectral bands drive each detector's decisions. Our results suggest that frequency-only detection is a viable, interpretable, and architecturally cheap alternative to semantic forensics for diffusion-era imagery, and that the isotropic-versus-anisotropic comparison is a scientifically meaningful axis rather than an engineering footnote.
\end{abstract}
